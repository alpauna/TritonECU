TRITON ECU - v1 CARRIER BOARD
1999 Ford F-150 4WD 5.4 V8 - engine AND transmission, one board
===============================================================================

  v1 is a CARRIER for a Nucleo-144, not a raw-chip board. The Nucleo plugs in
  through the ST morpho headers (2 x 2x35) and brings ST-Link, USB console and
  Ethernet with it, so the MCU section stops being a fabrication risk.
  See carrier-board.md.

  The design intent lives in docs/. This file is the wiring view of it; every
  value here has a document behind it and the citations are inline.

  ** PIN NAMES AND NUMBERS ARE FUNCTIONAL. ** Check them against each
  datasheet before drawing. This is intent, not a transcription.

1. POWER CHAIN
--------------

  TWO feeds, and only one of them is key-switched:

   VPWR  o--[ F1 ]--[SMDJ43A]--+---------------------------+
   pins 71, 97, circuit 361 RD |                           |
   EEC relay - DIES AT KEY-OFF |                           |
                            LTC4364-2                    (protected rail)
                          + pass FET                          |
                          + ideal-diode FET                   |
                                 |                            |
   BATTERY POST                                               |
   |                                                          |
   [F2 2A, within 150-300 mm of the post - protects the WIRE]  |
   |  18 AWG, loomed, grommeted at the firewall               |
   [SMDJ43A]-----[D3]--[R7 470R]--+--------------+------------+
                                  |              |
                              [SMBJ30A]          |
                                  |              |
                                 PGND            |
                                 +--------------+-------------+
                                                |             |
                                          MAX25239AFFA    NCV8772CDT504RKG
                                          buck-boost       5.00 V LDO
                                          2.1 MHz, 2.2 uH       |
                                          spread spectrum       |
                                                |               +--> VREF chain, sec.10
                                             +5.0 V
                                                |
                                       +--------+--------+
                                       |                 |
                                  TLV62085 buck      74HCT541 x3
                                    -> +3.3 V        TBD62083A
                                       |             DRV8837
                                  Nucleo, ADC,       (all 5 V loads)
                                  everything logic
                                       |
                                  MAX6070AAUT25
                                  2.5 V reference, GATED
                                       |
                                       +--> ADS8588H REFIO

  ** THE VREF LDO IS FED FROM THE PROTECTED RAIL, NOT THE 5 V SWITCHER. **
  The switcher has no headroom above 5.0 V and is spread-spectrum; a sensor
  reference wants neither. vref-supply.md.

  MAX25239 `SYNC` is a GPIO, not a strap - fit a pulldown so cold power-up
  lands in skip mode. power-supply.md.

  ** KAPWR JOINS THE PROTECTED RAIL, NOT THE LTC4364's INPUT. ** That one
  choice is the whole always-on design:

    - the LTC4364 is UNPOWERED when parked, so its 750 uA - 80% of the old
      sleep budget - is gone. 944 uA becomes 194 uA, 0.2%/month of a group
      65 battery instead of 1.0%.
    - back-feed out through pins 71/97 into the open relay contact is
      blocked by the ideal-diode FET that is already there.
    - R7 makes the OR asymmetric without an OR controller. KAPWR is if
      anything HIGHER than VPWR (which comes through a relay contact), so a
      plain diode-OR would run the whole ECU off the keep-alive fuse.
      470R against the LTC4364 path's ~20 mohm is 23500:1 - at 1 A of load
      KAPWR contributes 43 uA. It also caps KAPWR fault current at 30 mA.

  ** THE ALWAYS-ON FEED IS A DEDICATED BATTERY LEAD, NOT EEC-V PIN A-44. **
  It shares nothing with the truck's keep-alive loads, reads battery
  voltage without the BJB's drops in the way, and - usefully - uses no
  EEC-V pin at all, which retires the A-xx / 1-104 numbering [CONFIRM]
  that was blocking this drawing.

  ** THE BATTERY DIVIDER IS NOT ON THIS LEAD AT ALL - IT IS ON PIN 55. **
  See sec.4c. Downstream of D3 and R7 it would read 0.48 V LOW when parked
  with a LOAD-DEPENDENT correction; upstream it reads true battery but ties
  the measurement to the supply's own fuse. Pin 55 gives true battery on a
  SEPARATE fuse and leaves this lead carrying supply current only.

  ** THE VALUE CHANGED WITH THE NODE: 180k/30k, ratio 7.000. ** On the
  battery lead the worst case is the SMDJ43A's 69.4 V clamp, not the
  LTC4364's 27 V. At the old 150k/33k the channel would go OVER RANGE at
  12.5 V during a load dump. 180k/30k lands at 9.914 V, clips at 70 V just
  above where the TVS clamps, and is better on drain AND Zsrc too.
  adc-front-end.md owns the value.

  ** THE TWO READINGS NOW STRADDLE THE EEC RELAY. ** Divider = battery,
  INA238 = protected rail. The difference under load is the drop across the
  relay contact and the 361 RD harness run - corroded contacts become
  measurable instead of inferred. always-on-domain.md.

  ** THE LEAD IS ALWAYS HOT AND BYPASSES EVERY SWITCH IN THE VEHICLE. **
  Fuse at the post, not on the board: the fuse protects the WIRE, and R7
  already caps board fault current at 30 mA. Ground stays single-point on
  the EEC-V PWRGND pins - do NOT run a second ground to the battery
  negative. The reading therefore includes the ground-path drop and reads
  low while cranking, which is what the ECU's supply actually sees.


2. THE NUCLEO, AND WHAT THE CARRIER OWES IT
-------------------------------------------

  STM32F767ZI, ~114 I/O, 76 used, 36 spare after SWD. pin-budget.md.

  ** Remove both CN2 jumpers ** to disconnect the on-board ST-Link, and
  ** bring SWD out to a header on the carrier ** - four pins, and it means a
  failed Nucleo does not take the debug path with it.
  [CONFIRM] the CN2 designation against UM1974 for your board revision.

  Pins to avoid on the morpho headers are listed in carrier-board.md - PA7 is
  RMII_CRS_DV on this part, which rules out Arduino-header SPI.

  The SD card is a module wired to the morpho headers; the Nucleo has no
  socket. SDMMC, 4-bit - the 1-bit compromise was a 40-pin artefact and is
  no longer needed.


3. VR INPUTS - 2 x MAX9926, MODE A2
-----------------------------------

  Four channels, three used, one spare. No expander, no comparator discretes.

    CKP  (crank, 36-1)  --+
    CMP  (cam)          --+--> 2 x MAX9926 --> 4 x native EXTI
    OSS  (output shaft) --+      Mode A2
    TSS  (transfer case)--+

  CMP is **VR, single-ended** - measured at 371 ohms, closing the open
  question that had it down as a Hall part. review-vr-chain.md.

  ** Terminate the CMP shield at EEC-V pin 25 ** - and note pin 25 was
  mislabelled in an earlier revision of the pinout. review-vr-chain.md.


4. ANALOG INPUTS - ADS8588H
---------------------------

  8 channels, 16-bit, 500 kSPS simultaneous, +/-10 V, 9 kV clamp.
  Bench part is a Tokmas AD7606BSTZ in the same LQFP-64.

    SPI   SCK, MISO, CS, CONVST, BUSY   5 native pins
    static RESET, FRSTDATA, OS0-2, RANGE  6 native pins

  ** The six static pins are NATIVE. ** They were going onto a 5 V expander,
  whose output would have exceeded the ADC's DVDD+0.3 absolute maximum.
  Dropping the chain removed the conflict rather than working around it.
  review-expander-chain.md sections 2 and superseded header.

  DVDD = 3.3 V. VIH = 0.8 x DVDD = 2.64 V, met by a 3.3 V GPIO with margin.

  ** The ADS8588H is a 125 C part. ** An earlier note said 85 C and was being
  used to rule out under-hood mounting; that was the AD7606B bench part.
  review-expander-chain.md section 8.


4a. O2 SIGNAL PATH - FOUR BUFFERED CHANNELS
-------------------------------------------

  A HEGO is NOT a 0-5 V sensor. It is a galvanic cell with tens of kohm of
  source impedance hot, rising into the megohms cold or aged, and both
  converters LOAD it. review-o2-chain.md section 1. So these four channels
  do not connect straight to the ADC the way TPS/MAP/MAF do.

  Per channel, x4 (EEC-V pins 87, 60, 61, 35):

    pin --[R1 10k]--+--[C1 1n]-- AGND
                    |
                    +--[D1 BAT54S]--+-- AGND
                    |               +-- +5VA        clamp ~5.3 V
                    |
                    +--[R2 22M]----------> 455 mV BIAS NODE (shared)
                    |
                    |  << guarded high-Z node - guard ring off the buffer >>
                    |
                    +---->|+\
                          |  >--+--[R3 1k]--+--[C2 1u]-- AGND    159 Hz
                    +---->|-/   |           |
                    |           |           +-----------> ADC channel
                    +-----------+

    U1, U2   OPA2376AQDRQ1  SOIC-8 x2, AEC-Q100 Grade 1, 4 amps total

  ** D1 conducts at 5.3 V, BELOW the op-amp's internal 5.6 V ESD diode. **
  That is the whole reason for Schottky here: on a short to battery the
  BAT54S takes the current and the op-amp's ESD structure never turns on.
  R1 bounds ISO 7637-2 pulse 1 at -100 V to 10.0 mA. o2-input-stage.md s.2.

  ** R3 FIRST, then C2 to ground. ** The op-amp drives the resistor and
  never sees the capacitor. Reversed, it oscillates. o2-input-stage.md s.6.

  Where they land:

    87  LF bank 1 upstream    ADS8588H ch 2
    60  RF bank 2 upstream    ADS8588H ch 3
    61  LR bank 1 downstream  STM32 internal ADC
    35  RR bank 2 downstream  STM32 internal ADC

  JP, a 0R pad NOT POPULATED, links the high-Z node to the ADC side and
  bypasses the buffer for a fixed 0-5 V wideband module. It costs nothing
  and it is why the wideband decision does not have to be made now.


4b. THE BIAS NODE IS ALSO THE EXCITATION SOURCE
-----------------------------------------------

    +5VA --[91k 1%]--+--[9.1k 1%]-- AGND      455 mV, Zsrc 8.3k, 50 uA
                     |
     DAC --[R6 1k]---+--[100n]-- AGND
                     |
                     +---> R2 of all four channels

  The 22M bias resistors exist for open-circuit detection. They are also a
  known injection path into every cell at once. Move the bias node and:

      dV / dVb = Rt / (R2 + Rt)        Rt = Rs + R1
      Rt       = R2 . k / (1 - k)      k  = dV/dVb

  ** The cell's own EMF cancels exactly. ** So does op-amp offset, offset
  drift, and the divider's tolerance - they are common to both states.
  No stable mixture, no warm engine and no known lambda required.

    DAC 0.455 -> 3.000 V,  attenuated 8.3k/(1k+8.3k) = 0.8925
    dVb = 2271 mV at the node,  ~115 nA peak into the cell

    cell Rs      dV seen    disturbs a 1 V swing by
      10k         2.06 mV        0.2 %
      30k         4.12 mV        0.4 %     healthy, hot
     100k        11.30 mV        1.1 %
     300k        31.56 mV        3.2 %     the OBD-II failing row
       1M        99.70 mV       10.0 %
     open      2271    mV         -        unmistakable

  ** The disturbance self-scales inversely with how much the reading is
  worth ** - smallest on a healthy cell, largest on one whose reading was
  already useless.

  ** DAC at reset is high-Z, so the node sits at 455 mV exactly as it
  would without it. ** Firmware that never touches the DAC changes nothing.
  Fail-safe by omission.

  Modulate at 50 Hz and demodulate synchronously - a single before/after
  pair loses to the cell's own 50 V/s slewing by 24x. 50 Hz sits between
  the cell's 1-10 Hz switching and the 159 Hz R3/C2 corner (4.6 % loss).
  25 cycles = 0.5 s = 0.9 uV of noise against a 4.12 mV worst case.

  ** MEASURE IN THE HEATER PWM's OFF-WINDOW. ** Heater current shares
  harness ground with the cell. Section 7 PWMs the heaters for the
  ceramic's sake; that is what creates the windows this needs, and this
  is what lets that ramp close its loop. Neither was designed for the
  other. o2-input-stage.md section 7.


4c. BATTERY VOLTAGE SENSE - EEC-V PIN 55
---------------------------------------

  Circuit 729 RD/WH, Central Junction Box, HOT AT ALL TIMES, 5 A. This
  truck's keep-alive pin. The pinout calls it "B+ KAM", which is why it went
  unnoticed for so long - KAM is Keep Alive Memory, which is KAPWR.

   pin 55 --+--[SMBJ30A]-- PGND         same part as the lead's TVS3
            |
            +--[180k 1%]--+--[30k 1%]-- AGND
                          |
                          +------------> ADS8588H       ratio 7.000

  ** PIN 55 SENSES, THE BATTERY LEAD SUPPLIES. ** Separately fused - 5 A in
  the CJB, 2 A at the post - so measurement and power do not share a single
  failure. It also gives a permanently hot pin a job, and "unconnected" was
  always the weaker answer for a pin that is live at our connector whether
  we use it or not.

  ** THE CJB OBJECTION APPLIED TO AMPS AND THERE ARE NONE. ** Pin 55 was
  ruled out as a POWER source because it reaches the PCM through the Central
  Junction Box. As a sense-only input the divider draws 66.7 uA:

      0.16 ohm of fuse + contacts + wire  ->  10.7 uV
      1.00 ohm, absurdly corroded         ->  66.7 uV

  Parked drain does not grow, it moves: 117 uA on the lead, 67 uA on pin 55,
  184 uA total as before.

  ** THE FAILURE MODE, AND HANDLING IT IS THE DESIGN. ** Sense and supply
  are independent now, so ONE CAN FAIL WITHOUT THE OTHER. If the CJB fuse
  blows, pin 55 floats, the 30k leg pulls the node to 0 V, and BATTERY READS
  ZERO WHILE THE ECU IS STILL RUNNING off the dedicated lead. Left alone,
  the low-battery self-disable parks the ECU on a good battery - the exact
  failure the divider was moved to avoid, by a different route.

  Cross-check against the INA238, which reads the PROTECTED RAIL and is
  never fed by 729:

      pin 55    rail     diagnosis
      >11 V     >11 V    normal
      <2 V      >11 V    ** 729 OPEN / CJB FUSE BLOWN ** - not a flat battery
      <11.5 V   <11.5 V  genuinely low battery -> self-disable

  ** THE SELF-DISABLE REQUIRES AGREEMENT, NOT JUST A LOW READING. **
  Disagreement is a wiring fault: set a code, do not park the ECU. Allow
  ~1 V of slack - parked, the rail sits 0.48 V below the lead through D3
  and R7.

  ** THIS CHANNEL KEEPS THE ADS8588H's 9 kV CLAMP ** because it is
  UNBUFFERED, unlike the four O2 channels of sec.4a. The 180k top leg holds
  the harness to <= 0.5 mA at any plausible transient (-100 V pulse 1 gives
  -476 uA), so the clamp is never stressed. o2-input-stage.md had to add a
  BAT54S only because a buffer relocated that boundary; here it never moved.

  adc-front-end.md owns the divider value. findings sec.19.
  calc/pin55_sense.py.


5. IGNITION - 8 x COIL ON PLUG
------------------------------

   GPIO x8 --> 74HCT541 #1 @ 5 V --> 470R --> ISL9V3040 x8 --> coils

  470 ohm is the datasheet's own switching condition at VGE = 5 V, and 70 ohm
  of that is already internal to the part. output-drivers.md.

  ** THE BUFFER HAS TWO ENABLES, AND THEY MEAN DIFFERENT THINGS: **

    OE1  (pin 1)   firmware GPIO      "engine position is known, ignition armed"
    OE2  (pin 19)  hardware watchdog  "firmware is still alive"

  Both active-low, ANDed internally. A supervisor (TPS3823-class) drives OE2.
  ** If the watchdog is deferred, fit the footprint and strap OE2 LOW. **

  Coil primary inductance is unmeasured and sets the dwell limit against the
  ISL9V3040's 300 mJ rating. One of the two genuinely open items - sec.14.


6. INJECTION - 8 x SEQUENTIAL
-----------------------------

   GPIO x8 --> ZXMS6005DGQ x8 --> injectors

  Direct from GPIO, no buffer. Full sequential - the semi-sequential fallback
  was a 40-pin compromise and is not needed.


7. SOLENOIDS AND HEATERS
------------------------

   GPIO x8 --> 74HCT541 #3 @ 5 V --> NCV8405ASTT1G x8   ** FULL, 8 of 8 **

     HO2S heater x4   pins 93, 94, 95, 96   - PWM them, see below
     canister vent    pin 67   - found reading Ford's diagrams; in no earlier list
     IMCC             pin 46   [CONFIRM] leans on/off
     SSA, SSB         pins 27, 1

  [CONFIRM] ** CSS may not exist. ** The coast-clutch solenoid came from the
  MegaSquirt sheet and is not on Ford's 4R70W diagram, which shows only SSA and
  SSB at the transmission connector. output-drivers.md channel budget.

  ** The buffer is a VOLTAGE translator, not a pin saver. ** The NCV8405A's
  RDS(on) is specified at VGS = 5 V and the STM32 is a 3.3 V part. This
  buffer survived the expander chain's removal because it was never the
  expander's to justify.

  74HCT541 #3's `OE` shares the watchdog net that drives #1's `OE2` - one
  net, no extra pin, and the shutdown path becomes redundant rather than
  serial.

  ** PWM THE O2 HEATERS - AND THE DRIVER IS NOT THE REASON. **

  The driver copes unaided: current limit at 6 A IS a soft start, and firmware
  must not read it as a fault. But while coping it drops whatever the cold PTC
  does not - 16 W at a cold resistance 1/5 of hot, 49 W at 1/10 - in a SOT-223,
  for hundreds of milliseconds, four channels, every cold start. Steady state
  is 0.47 W.

  The real reason is the SENSOR. A cold zirconia element with exhaust
  condensation on it cracks when heated fast. So:

    1. DELAY turn-on after a cold start (time or CHT)   protects the sensor
    2. RAMP duty rather than stepping to 100%           keeps the driver out
                                                        of current limit
    3. STAGGER the four channels                        24 A becomes 10.5 A

  None of it changes the hardware. output-drivers.md.

  [MEASURE] the cold resistance of one sensor - 1/5 against 1/10 of hot is the
  difference between 16 W and 49 W in the driver.

  NCV8405A carries drain-sense diagnosis. The divider that reads it cannot be
  sized against a 5 V VIH of 4.0 V - "there are no values that work" - which
  is the same 0.8*VDD arithmetic that later killed the expander chain.
  output-drivers.md.


8. PWM LOADS
------------

   native timer pins x5 --> 74HCT541 #2 @ 5 V --> drivers

     EVAP purge     NCV8405A      pin 56
     EGR regulator  NCV8405A      pin 47
     TCC            NCV8405A      pin 54
     EPC            NCV8408B      pin 81, DPAK, >= 200 Hz
     IAC            NCV8405A      pin 83, freewheel to VPWR, no new pin
     VSS out        NCV8405A      pin 68 - see sec.8a
                                  6 of 8 used, 2 spare

  ** PWM CANNOT LIVE ON A BUS. ** An earlier revision put EVAP and EGR on the
  expander because the split was made by current and criticality, and PWM is
  neither. Every edge would have been a bus transaction. review-solenoid-chain.md.

  74HCT541 #2's `OE` is a GPIO, not the watchdog - it has to be releasable
  under software control, unlike #1 and #3.


8a. VSS OUT, AND TWO RESERVED PINS THAT STOP HERE
-------------------------------------------

   GPIO --> 74HCT541 #2 ch6 --> NCV8405A #13 --> EEC-V pin 68 (679 GY/BK)
                                  open drain          |
                                                      +--> speed control servo C157
      [ pull-up footprint, NOT POPULATED ]             +--> GEM C267
                                                      +--> rear air suspension C277

  ** NOT on 74HCT541 #3. ** #3 is full at 8, so the obvious home for a slow
  output has no room. #2 has the space.

  ** OPEN DRAIN, PULL-UP UNPOPULATED. ** The open [CONFIRM] is what those three
  modules expect on 679 - 12 V, 5 V or open-drain. Open-drain with no pull-up
  is safe under ALL THREE readings, because the receiving modules set their own
  high level. Populate the pull-up only if measurement shows they need a driven
  high.

  The ordering is the point: driving 12 V into a module that pulls up to 5 V is
  the failure mode, and it is only reachable by populating before measuring.

  ~156 Hz at 8000 pulses/mile and 70 mph. [CONFIRM] the pulses-per-mile for
  this truck - it sets the firmware scaling, not the driver choice.

  ** THERE IS NO DISCRETE TACH OUTPUT. ** The cluster takes engine speed over
  SCP and has no pin for it. schematic-findings.md section 12.

  TWO RESERVED SQUARE-WAVE OUTPUTS, on a header, for a scope or a bench gauge:

   GPIO --[ series R ]--> TACH  --+
                                  |   4-pin header, 3.3 V logic
   GPIO --[ series R ]--> SPEED --+   GND at both ends for probing
                                      ** STOPS HERE **

  ** NEITHER REACHES THE EEC-V CONNECTOR. ** And SPEED is NOT the VSS output
  above - that one is NCV8405A #13 to pin 68, open-drain, feeding three OEM
  modules. This is a separate signal at logic level carrying the same
  information, for something that wants a clean edge rather than Ford's.

  GPIOs are cheap against 34 spare, so both are reserved now rather than
  needing a respin later. Expanding either costs one channel on #2 (2 spare
  after VSS), one NCV8405A and a connector pin - and the connector pin is the
  part to check first, since the OEM pinout has no tach assignment to inherit
  and pin 68 is already committed.


9. RELAYS AND LAMPS
-------------------

   GPIO x4 --> TBD62083AFNG --> fuel pump, fan 1, fan 2, A/C clutch

  8-channel DMOS sink with clamps built in. VIN(ON) = 2.5 V at 100 uA, so it
  takes a 3.3 V GPIO directly - no buffer, unlike the NCV8405A gates.

  RON 1.14 ohm worst case, not a Darlington's 1.1 V saturation drop: 46 mW per
  channel at a 200 mA coil, against 220 mW for a ULN2803. output-drivers.md.

  Fan 2 is the second cooling fan - cooling-fans.md. Both relays are native
  pins now.


10. VREF OUTPUT CHAIN - TWO 5 V SENSOR FEEDS
--------------------------------------------

  Per feed, and there are two:

   NCV8772 5.00 V --> TPS2H160B-Q1 --> LM74700-Q1 + DMN6040SVTQ-7 --> SMAJ24CA
                      dual high-side     ideal diode                 bidirectional
                      250 mA limit,      (breaks the reverse         TVS, INBOARD
                      40 V, current      path rather than            of where the
                      sense              surviving it)               PPTC was

  Control: EN, IN1, IN2, DIAG_EN, SEL, FAULT - ** 6 native pins. ** These had
  no home in any document: vref-supply.md charged "6 GPIO" and stopped, and
  the 37-pin map never listed them. review-expander-chain.md section 3 found
  them.

  ** THER strapped HIGH - latch, not auto-retry. ** A sensor feed that
  retries into a short chatters the whole rail.

  R_CL 8.06k sets the current limit; R_CS 2.2k the sense ratio. ** The CS pin
  needs ADC protection ** - it can swing above the ADC's input range under
  fault. review-vref-chain.md.

  The PPTC was REMOVED entirely rather than kept as belt-and-braces: it was
  the slowest element in a chain whose whole point is speed.


11. SCP / J1850 PWM - THE ONLY PATH TO THE DASH
-----------------------------------------------

   TX_P  --+
   TX_N  --+--> DRV8837 H-bridge --> PWM+ / PWM-  (differential)
   RX    <---- TLV7031 comparator <--+
   nSLEEP ---> DRV8837                 5 V, no boost rail

  ** FOUR GPIOs, not three. ** nSLEEP releases the bus and was missed in the
  original budget. review-scp-chain.md section 1.

  ** This is not a convenience. ** Ford's cluster diagrams show the cluster's
  only connection to the PCM is SCP - the tachometer, speedometer and MIL have
  no discrete PCM pins. The choice was never "SCP or discrete wires", it was
  "SCP or no dash at all". schematic-findings.md section 10.

  Bus access is EEC-V pins 15 and 16 - SCP- (PNK/LT BLU, circuit 915) and
  SCP+ (TAN/ORG, circuit 914). The same wires as DLC pins 10 and 2, so one
  connection serves the cluster and a scan tool.

  ** Phase 0 must TRANSMIT into live traffic, not only listen. **
  review-scp-chain.md section 2. The reference design warns the PWM side is
  experimental and expects per-vehicle tuning; Phase 0 is where that happens.

  [CONFIRM] automotive qualification on both parts. The DRV8837 has a Q1
  variant; the TLV7031 wants checking.

-------------------

  104-pin, rusEFI footprint. Notable assignments established by review:

    ** Numbered FLAT 1-104, on a 4 x 26 physical layout. ** Do NOT wire from
    EEC-V-Power-Pins.png - that chart is a three-connector PCM and is not this
    vehicle. eec-v-pinout.md is the authority, corroborated by Ford's own EVTM

    15, 16   SCP- / SCP+                        sec.11

13. WHAT IS DELIBERATELY NOT ON THIS BOARD
------------------------------------------

  * ** No MCP23S17 expander chain. ** It existed because the ESP32-P4 had 40
    GPIOs and needed 39. On ~114 the split stops being necessary, and five of
    its six open findings evaporate with it.
  * ** No Wi-Fi. ** Dropped, not deferred - an always-on radio on an engine
    controller is a remote attack surface with a path to code execution via
    OTA. Ethernet does the job and needs physical access.
  * ** No second CPU and no CAN transmission node. ** That was an artefact of
    a 25-pin carrier. One board does engine and transmission.
  * No alternator control - the OEM alternator self-regulates.

  Footprints only, because they are cheap and save a respin:
    ADR4525 precision reference (+ REF SELECT strap), INA238-Q1 current

14a. CLOSED, AND WORTH KEEPING CLOSED
-------------------------------------

  * THE A-xx / 1-104 PIN MAPPING DOES NOT EXIST. EEC-V-Power-Pins.png
    describes a THREE-CONNECTOR PCM - its own text says "Connector A / B / C
    signal return" - and puts VPWR on ADJACENT pins A-32/A-33 where this
    truck's are 71 and 97, twenty-six apart. This truck has ONE 104-pin
    connector on a physical 4 x 26 layout: grounds 51/77/103 plus 3, VPWR
    71/97 in one column, coils in two complete columns.
    eec-v-pinout.md is the authority. calc/eec_v_numbering.py.

    Two conclusions that rested on that chart CHANGED:
      - Sensor returns are ONE SIGRTN (pin 91, circuit 359, spliced at
        S135/S138) PLUS a dedicated MAF return (pin 36, circuit 968). Not
        three branches meeting inside the module - the joining is done by
        harness splices. And pin 36 is a KELVIN SENSE wire, not a second
        star: the MAF's own 0 V is 570 BK/WH on the POWER ground net.
        Tying pin 36 to AGND at the connector throws that away.
      - Power grounds are FOUR: pins 3, 51, 77, 103, circuit 570 BK/WH, all
        0 V, joining at S100 and going to body ground G101. NOT five - an
        earlier revision counted pin 24, which the MegaSquirt sheet lists
        but which carries NO WIRE on this truck.

  * THERE IS NO CASE GROUND (CSEGND) ON THIS TRUCK. Those four pins are the
    PCM's complete ground complement. The chart's A-43 belongs to the other
    PCM. The ECU's shield bond is a MECHANICAL question - how the box is
    mounted and earthed - not a connector pin. enclosure.md.

  * FEPS / DLC PIN 13. EngineControls2.png traces it end to end: PCM pin 13
    -> 107 VT -> C158 -> C228F -> DLC pin 13. Leave it unconnected; nothing
    good happens if 18 V arrives on a 3.3 V system.

  * VPWR IS KEY-SWITCHED, PROVEN AT THE RELAY COIL. Fed from "hot in start
    or run" (16 RD/LG) through the PCM power diode, grounded at G104. No PCM
    output touches it, so there is no afterrun hold. findings sec.17.

  * PIN 55 IS THE BATTERY VOLTAGE SENSE PIN - sec.4c. findings sec.19.
